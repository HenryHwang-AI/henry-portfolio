别为病毒式传播优化：写 Agent，少做教程工厂 Don’t optimize for virality: write about agents without becoming tutorial spam

X / 短内容很容易把人训练成「教程工厂」：长帖、清单、第一句就回答「学到什么」。流量会来，但代价是——你开始为预估的阅读量选题，高质量却安静的帖子会让你焦虑，真正该做的 Demo 反而后排。

我改优化什么

我把目标改成对话与手艺，不是曝光。写投放作战室，我会写角色怎么吵、失败怎么复盘，而不是「十分钟搭 Multi-Agent」。写评测台，我会写指标为什么骗人，而不是再发一篇「Eval 最佳实践 20 条」。愿意回你、愿意追问边界的人，比一万次划过更值。

个人品牌不是印象数

品牌是别人想起你时，能不能准确说出你在做的窄东西。三个 Demo 就是那条窄东西。发内容时我自问：这是在加深那条线，还是在用通用模板换点赞？若是后者，就停。算法偶尔送一次触达；记得你、愿意一起拆问题的人，才是复利。

所以：发你真正好奇、真正踩过坑的；认真回帖；让交换长成关系。别成为「Agent 教程男」——那条路短、挤、而且会吃掉你真正想造的东西。

怎么写仍有信息量

不优化病毒，不等于写得含糊。我仍要求第一段说清楚「解决什么痛」，但拒绝为了完播率把每个坑都包装成通用 checklist。细节可以留在博客长文和 Demo README 里；短内容负责抛出一个真实问题，把愿意深聊的人引到可跑的东西上。

若某条帖子火了却带不回任何对三个 Demo 的追问，我会把它记成噪声，而不是成功。成功是有人复现了失败路径，或指出了我漏测的边界。

实操上，我会把「想发的清单体」先压成一句问题发到草稿，隔天若仍觉得必须展开成教程，再决定要不要写长文挂博客——多数时候那句问题本身就够，或者直接指向某个 Demo 的失败日志更有用。

X / short-form trains people into a “tutorial factory”: long posts, lists, first lines that answer “what will I learn.” Reach arrives—but you start picking topics for predicted views, quiet high-quality posts make you anxious, and the demos that matter slip to the back.

What I optimize instead

I optimize for conversations and craft, not impressions. On the war room I write how roles argue and how failures get reviewed—not “multi-agent in ten minutes.” On the eval lab I write why metrics lie—not another “20 eval best practices.” People who reply and pressure your edges beat ten thousand scrolls.

Brand isn’t impression count

Brand is whether someone can name the narrow thing you actually ship. The three demos are that narrow thing. Before posting I ask: does this deepen the line, or trade a generic template for likes? If the latter, stop. Algorithms gift reach once; people who remember you and dissect problems with you compound.

So: post what you’re curious about and scarred by; reply thoughtfully; let exchanges become relationships. Don’t become the “agent tutorial guy”—that road is short, crowded, and eats what you meant to build.

Still write with signal

Skipping virality isn’t an excuse to be vague. I still want the first paragraph to name the pain—but I refuse to wrap every scar into a generic checklist for completion rate. Detail can live in long posts and demo READMEs; short posts should pose a real question and route curious people to something runnable.

If a post goes wide yet brings zero follow-ups about the three demos, I log it as noise, not success. Success is someone replaying a failure path or naming an edge case I missed.

In practice I compress “listicle urges” into one draft question; if it still needs a tutorial the next day, I consider a long blog post—often the question alone is enough, or a pointer to a demo failure log is more useful.
