成本降了以后，我还在评什么 After cost falls, what I still evaluate

生成变便宜之后，我仍然要决定默认走哪一档，以及用什么口径验收。成本是路由条件。一次采纳率和复核通过率不因为单价下降就放宽。

默认低成本档，质量口径留在原处

AllyNext 按媒体类型分池：图像、视频、文本、内容理解。池内再按时长、速度和成本路由，默认走低成本档。按需求还可以分成 mini、fast 和标准。素材生成成本下降 60% 到 80%。

成本下来以后，我仍看三组彼此不同的口径。一次采纳率从 [30–50%] 到 [70–85%]，看的是生成结果能不能直接用，区间保留。视频制作从约 2 天到 20 分钟，Listing 生成从约 3 小时到 15 分钟，看的是履约耗时；简历把这两则概括为交付时间最高下降 99%，这是概括，不是另一项实验。配合大规模 A/B，产出素材平均 CTR 为 3%，同比提升 150%。这是配合，实验前的 CTR 绝对值没有给出。三组口径都不被成本降幅替代。

时间是 2025 年 3 月到 12 月。项目条目是 AI 产品负责人，工作经历是深度参与全链路。见 AllyNext。

选品侧只写得清目标，写不出成本结果

商舟按步骤取数，Context 只留当步商品和命中规则，节点之间传结构化中间结果。目标里包括降低 Token 成本和延迟。简历没有成本数字，也没有延迟数字。我不能把 AllyNext 的 60% 到 80% 挪到这条链路上，也不能另估一个倍数。能一起看的，仍是选品报告的人工复核通过率 95% 以上，以及关键结论 99% 的可追溯率。角色是 AI 产品负责人。见 商舟。

便宜改变的是默认档，不是验收线

四类媒体池把图像、视频、文本和内容理解分开路由，避免用同一种时长和成本假设去跑所有素材。mini、fast 和标准是按需求分的档，默认仍是低成本档。成本下降 60% 到 80% 说明素材生成的花费下来了。它不改写一次采纳率的区间，也不把「约 2 天到 20 分钟」「约 3 小时到 15 分钟」换成成本故事。CTR 的 3% 和同比 150% 仍是配合实验的点击口径。

商舟的 4 小时到 0.5 小时是同等商品规模下的选品耗时，降幅 87.5%，不是 Token 成本。Context 按步骤裁剪的目标继续单独写，没有数字就不补数字。人工操作 100 次以上到 9 次、复核通过率 95% 以上、可追溯率 99%，也都不改写成成本下降的证据。验收线因此还是：一次能不能被采纳，耗时落在哪个量级，配合实验时点击如何，以及在没有成本数字的链路上，复核和追溯是否还在。单价下降，不自动改写这些线。默认低成本档是路由选择，标准档留给需求本身要求更高的时候，两档共用同一套验收，而不是便宜的那档另设一条更松的线。AllyNext 上成本下降 60% 到 80% 的同时，一次采纳率仍用区间来写，视频和 Listing 的耗时仍用「约」来写。商舟上没有成本结果，就继续用复核、追溯和选品耗时来验收，不借用另一条产品的降幅。默认档可以更便宜，验收线仍是同一条。图像、视频、文本和内容理解分成不同的池之后，每一池仍按采纳、耗时和需求分档来看，不因为这一池更便宜就单独放行。

After generation gets cheaper, I still have to decide the default tier, and which measures accept the result. Cost is a routing condition. First-pass acceptance and review pass rate do not loosen because the unit price fell.

The default is the low-cost tier; the quality measures stay where they were

AllyNext pools by media type: image, video, text, and content understanding. Inside a pool, routing uses duration, speed, and cost, and the default is the low-cost tier. Demand can also be split into mini, fast, and standard. Asset-generation cost fell 60% to 80%.

After that drop I still read three measures that are not the same. First-pass acceptance moved from [30–50%] to [70–85%]. It asks whether a generated result can be used as-is, and I keep the ranges. Video production went from about 2 days to 20 minutes, and Listing generation from about 3 hours to 15 minutes. Those measure turnaround. The resume summarizes them as delivery time down by as much as 99%. That is a summary, not another experiment. Alongside large-scale A/B tests, average CTR of the produced assets was 3%, up 150% year over year. That was alongside the tests. The pre-test CTR is not given. None of the three is replaced by the size of the cost drop.

The dates are March to December 2025. The project entry says AI product owner; the work history says deep participation across the chain. See AllyNext.

On selection I can state the aim, not a cost result

Shangzhou fetches data by step. Context keeps only the current product and the rules that hit, and nodes pass structured intermediate results. The aims include lower token cost and lower latency. The resume has no cost figure and no latency figure. I cannot move AllyNext’s 60% to 80% onto this chain, and I do not estimate another multiple. What can still be read beside that aim is a 95%+ human review pass rate on selection reports, and 99% traceability of key conclusions. My role was AI product owner. See Shangzhou.

Cheaper changes the default tier, not the acceptance line

The four media pools route image, video, text, and content understanding apart, so one assumption about duration and cost is not applied to every asset. Mini, fast, and standard are tiers by demand. The default remains the low-cost tier. A 60% to 80% cost drop says asset generation spends less. It does not rewrite the first-pass range, and it does not turn “about 2 days to 20 minutes” or “about 3 hours to 15 minutes” into a cost story. CTR of 3% and the 150% year-over-year rise remain a click measure from work alongside an experiment.

Shangzhou’s 4 hours to 0.5 hours is selection time at the same product scale, down 87.5%. It is not a token cost. The aim of trimming context by step stays written on its own. Where there is no figure, I do not add one. Manual operations from 100+ to 9, review pass rate of 95%+, and traceability of 99% are not rewritten as evidence of a cost drop either. The acceptance line is therefore still: whether a result can be taken on the first pass, what magnitude the turnaround lands on, how clicks look alongside an experiment, and, on the chain with no cost figure, whether review and trace are still there. A lower unit price does not rewrite those lines by itself. The low-cost default is a routing choice. The standard tier is for demand that itself asks for more. Both tiers share the same acceptance line. The cheaper tier does not get a looser one. On AllyNext, while cost fell 60% to 80%, first-pass acceptance is still written as a range, and the video and Listing durations still keep the “about.” On Shangzhou, where there is no cost result, acceptance stays with review, trace, and selection time. I do not borrow the other product’s drop. The default tier can be cheaper. The acceptance line stays the same one. After image, video, text, and content understanding are split into pools, each pool is still read by acceptance, turnaround, and the tier the demand needs. A cheaper pool is not waved through on price alone.
