先把问题说清楚，再决定 Agent 做哪一段 Name the problem before you assign any step to an agent

我做 AI 产品，最先要拍板的是问题本身：这段工作里，哪一块可以交给自动，哪一块必须有人点头。模型可以换，问题没说清，后面的节点都会切错。

先把「看不过来」写成缺一层翻译

2023 年 7 月到 12 月，我以 AI 产品经理的角色执行落地经营分析 Copilot。运营每天面对原报表里的 50 多个指标，看不过来，看完也不知道下一步做什么。我调研了 4 个部门、40 位资深运营，把痛点定在「指标到动作」之间缺一层翻译。产品按这个定义来做：健康度只留 8 个核心指标，异常归因去找拖分项，再给动作建议。日报和周报由模型生成，并支持自然语言问数和主动提醒。

运营使用日活从 31% 到 90% 以上，业务流程耗时减少 70%，使用范围到了 1000 多名运营。日活的分母没有另行写出，我把它当作使用口径，不把它讲成和旧报表的对照实验。异常归因准确率 95% 以上，也没有对照基线。耗时、日活、归因准确率各量各的。项目记录在 经营分析 Copilot。

路线按能不能验收来选

AllyNext 的时间是 2025 年 3 月到 12 月。项目条目写的是 AI 产品负责人，工作经历里的对应说法是深度参与全链路，两处我都保留。要生产的是 Listing、主图、A+ 和视频，人工协同重，工具链是断开的。通用大模型缺少域内知识，也缺少按 SOP 编排的能力，稳定交不出可投产的交付物。我对比过更自主的 Agent 等方案，原则是可控、可验收、成本低，决策采用 Workflow、单 Agent 和技能包。取舍过程写在 AllyNext。

流程切在确定性和风险上

商舟从 2026 年 2 月做到简历所记的 2026 年 11 月 15 日，我的角色是 AI 产品负责人。批量选品的数据分散，判断依赖个人经验，评估标准不统一，所以周期长，结论也难再复现。我主导设计了九步选品流程的方案和原型，按确定性和风险把节点分成 Workflow、Agent 决策和人工确认，并把利润与合规评估放到前面。同等商品规模下，单次选品从 4 小时降到 0.5 小时，降幅 87.5%。这个数字量的是耗时。节点如何留人，记在 商舟。

切分要能交出去做

这三次判断，我都要求落到一份可以交给研发和运营的切分。Copilot 的切分是三段：8 个核心指标上的健康度、拖分项上的归因、然后是动作建议。50 多个指标仍在原来的报表里，产品不再把它们一次摊开。AllyNext 的切分是一条生产链路：Workflow 负责编排，单 Agent 按技能包推进，技能包承接运营 SOP。更自主的方案被放在对照里，主方案没有采用它。商舟的切分写进九步流程的方案和原型：Workflow 节点、Agent 决策节点、人工确认节点分开标，利润和合规评估放在 Listing 生成之前。

日活、耗时和降幅各自留在自己的项目里。我不用商舟的 87.5% 去解释 Copilot 的日活，也不用 Copilot 的 90% 以上去证明 AllyNext 的路线选对了。能并排看的，是三次都先把问题收成一个可执行的切分。模型名称不在这些决定的中心。

调研的规模就是 4 个部门、40 位资深运营。我用它支持「痛点在翻译层」这个定义。路线对比只保留原则：可控、可验收、成本低。九步流程是我主导的方案和原型，它回答节点怎么切，并把利润与合规评估放在前面。

The decision I make first on an AI product is the problem itself: which part of the work can run on its own, and which part still needs a person to agree. Models can be swapped. If the problem is vague, the later nodes get cut in the wrong place.

Rewrite “too much to read” as a missing translation

From July to December 2023 I executed an operations-analysis Copilot as an AI product manager. Operators faced 50+ metrics on the old reports. They could not get through them, and reading them still did not say what to do next. After interviews with 40 senior operators in 4 departments, I set the pain as a missing translation from metric to action. The product follows that definition: a health view keeps 8 core metrics, anomaly attribution finds what drags the score, and then comes a suggested action. Daily and weekly reports are generated by the model, with natural-language questions and proactive reminders.

Operations daily active use went from 31% to 90%+. Process time fell 70%. The rollout reached 1,000+ operators. The denominator of daily active use is not separately defined, so I read it as a usage figure, not as a controlled comparison with the old reports. Anomaly-attribution accuracy is 95%+ with no baseline. Time, daily use, and attribution accuracy each measure their own thing. The project write-up is the operations Copilot.

Pick the route by whether it can be accepted

AllyNext ran from March to December 2025. The project entry lists me as AI product owner; the work history says I took part deeply across the chain. I keep both wordings. The work was Listing copy, main images, A+ content, and video, with heavy human coordination and a broken toolchain. A general model lacked domain knowledge and the ability to follow an SOP, so it could not steadily hand over something fit to publish. I compared more autonomous agent options and, on the rule of controllable, acceptable, and lower cost, chose a workflow, a single agent, and a skill pack. That choice is written up in AllyNext.

Cut the process on certainty and risk

Shangzhou ran from February 2026 through the resume date of 15 November 2026. My role was AI product owner. Batch product selection had scattered data, personal experience, and uneven standards, so cycles were long and a conclusion was hard to run again. I led the design of a nine-step selection flow and its prototype, split nodes into workflow, agent decision, and human confirmation by certainty and risk, and moved profit and compliance checks forward. At the same product scale, one selection pass went from 4 hours to 0.5 hours, down 87.5%. That number measures time. How people stay on the nodes is in Shangzhou.

The cut has to be something others can build

Each of the three judgments had to land as a cut I could hand to engineering and operations. The Copilot’s cut is three parts: health on 8 core metrics, attribution of what drags the score, then a suggested action. The 50+ metrics stay in the old reports. The product no longer lays them out all at once. AllyNext’s cut is a production chain: the workflow orchestrates, one agent moves through a skill pack, and the skill pack carries the operations SOP. More autonomous options sat in the comparison. The main design did not take them. Shangzhou’s cut is written into the nine-step scheme and prototype: workflow nodes, agent-decision nodes, and human-confirmation nodes are marked apart, and profit and compliance review sit before Listing generation.

Daily use, time, and the percentage drop each stay with their own project. I do not use Shangzhou’s 87.5% to explain the Copilot’s daily use, and I do not use the Copilot’s 90%+ to prove AllyNext’s route. What can sit side by side is that each time the problem was first gathered into a cut someone could build. The model name is not the center of these decisions.

The interviews are 4 departments and 40 senior operators. I use that scale to support the definition that the pain sits in the translation layer. The route comparison keeps its rule: controllable, acceptable, and lower cost. The nine-step flow is a scheme and prototype I led. It answers how the nodes are cut, and it places profit and compliance review first.
