为什么要写下来：笔记与博客是产品思维的外置缓存 Why write things down: notes and posts as external cache for product thinking

脑子里的产品直觉像未落盘的缓存：当时很亮，隔一周只剩「好像想过」。写下来不是为了看起来勤奋，而是把模糊判断变成可检查的句子——别人能反驳，未来的自己也能改。

和第二大脑、博客怎么接

笔记页对我是低摩擦入口：Inbox 收碎片，链到场景与决策，而不是先建完美库。博客则是「公开过一遍」的压力测试：写评测台时，我不得不说清「好用」到底指完成率、幻觉率还是人工改写成本；写 RAG 时，不得不说清引用挂在哪一层。说不清的地方，往往就是产品还没想透的地方。

写，是为了想清楚

我很少为了更新频率硬写。更常是：Demo 卡在一个选择（要不要 GUI 兜底？评测要不要拆脆弱依赖？），先写一段给自己看。写完要么动手改架构，要么删掉幻想需求。公开博客只是其中一部分；日记式私有笔记同样算——关键是外置，不是表演。

站上的「笔记」入口，就是提醒自己：收集 → 链接 → 偶尔写成文。少建库，多写能被下周的自己读懂的话。产品思维需要带宽；纸和文件是便宜的 SRAM。

从碎片到可决策句子

Inbox 里常有「评测要加个维度」这种半句话。若不写完，它会永远停在情绪层。我逼自己补成：维度叫什么、怎么采样、和业务指标如何对齐、会不会奖励刷屏式工具调用。补完往往发现：要么已经有现成字段，要么这个维度根本测不到——于是删需求比加仪表盘更快。

博客公开版是精修后的结论；私有笔记保留吵架过程。两者一起，才构成我所谓的外置缓存：热数据在工作集里，冷数据可检索，而不是全堆在脑子的 HBM 幻想里。

Product intuition in your head is like an unsaved cache: bright in the moment, a week later only “I think I had something.” Writing isn’t performative diligence—it’s turning fuzzy judgment into checkable sentences others can argue with and future-you can edit.

How it ties to notes and the blog

The notes page is a low-friction inbox: capture scraps, link to scenes and decisions, skip the perfect database first. The blog is a public stress test: writing the eval lab forced me to define “usable” as completion, hallucination, or rewrite cost; writing RAG forced me to say where citations attach. Whatever I can’t say clearly is usually where the product isn’t clear yet.

Write to think

I rarely write for cadence. More often a demo stalls on a choice (GUI fallback? score fragile deps separately?), and I draft a paragraph for myself. Afterward I either change the architecture or delete a fantasy requirement. Public posts are only one layer; private journal notes count—the point is externalization, not performance.

The site’s Notes entry is a reminder: capture → link → occasionally publish. Fewer databases; more sentences next-week-you can parse. Product thinking needs bandwidth; paper and files are cheap SRAM.

From scraps to decidable sentences

The inbox often holds half-lines like “add an eval dimension.” Left unfinished, they stay emotional. I force a full form: name the dimension, sampling, business alignment, and whether it rewards spammy tool calls. Finishing usually shows we already have the field—or the dimension isn’t measurable—so deleting the ask beats adding a dashboard.

Public posts hold the polished conclusion; private notes keep the argument. Together they are the external cache I mean: hot data in the working set, cold data retrievable—not everything imagined into mental HBM.
